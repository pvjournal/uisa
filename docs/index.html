Universal Information Structure Analysis

Toward carrier-agnostic discovery of recoverable structure in digital artifacts. Abhishek Choudhary.

Read the record (PDF, 18 pages)

The question

Can an automated system discover statistically defensible, representation-independent evidence of recoverable information structure in arbitrary digital artifacts, and progressively infer the representation or channel through which that structure can be decoded?

An observable artifact does not come with its representation system attached. A byte sequence can be text, samples, pixels, instructions or records; a dancer's performance can carry information in joint angles, gaze, stage position or timing against the music. UISA asks which representation makes the information-bearing structure recoverable, without assuming the carrier, the alphabet, the synchronisation or the modality in advance.

What the record contains

  1. The inverse problem stated, and why information is not identical to its carrier.
  2. Related work: Shannon information theory, minimum description length, algorithmic information, atypicality, universal and multimodal steganalysis, representation learning, blind source separation.
  3. A conceptual model from artifact to canonical information, and five propositions with proofs: finite-family error control, a complexity-weighted discovery bound, recoverability requiring retained information, search order as a resource variable, and residual search.
  4. Statistical foundations and the null-model problem; the architecture (detect, represent, localise the channel, decode, validate, subtract, search again).
  5. A prototype and its benchmark, a negative result and what it means, representation discovery as the core problem, multiplexed and hierarchical channels, arbitrary alphabets.
  6. Human and AI research method and accountability, computational complexity, limitations, a roadmap, and appendices on reproduction, data schema, provenance and responsible research.
